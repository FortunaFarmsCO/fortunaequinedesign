My approach

See the space the way your horse will.

An efficient, functional layout is a top priority. Horse welfare and happiness is equally important. To get both, you have to picture each space from the horse’s perspective and walk through how the people will use it with the horses, every single day.

“Each barn design and layout has to be tailored to the owner and how they plan to work with and care for their horses.”

How I help

Bring me in at any stage, from first sketch to final walk-through.

I’m not an architect. As an expert in stable design, I work alongside your architect and builder, focused on how you want to use the space with your horses.

Barn Layout

A careful look at your plans: stall placement, aisle flow, wash and tack areas, feed and hay, and the daily chore path.

Site Planning

The property layout is as important as the design of the barn. How will the horses get to and from the pastures or turnouts? How will equipment get around?

Plan for Your Weather

What works in which climate, drawn from keeping horses in five very different places.

Ventilation & Air Flow

Good ventilation is key for a clean stable and your horses’ health. Hot air and ammonia gases need to vent out, and clean air needs to be able to come in.

Castle Rock, Colorado

Priorities

  • Health first. Powered, adjustable louvers run the length of the roof just under the skylights to exhaust hot air and ammonia.
  • Clean air in. Intake louvers above each stall’s back door, under the overhang, keep fresh air moving through passive air exchange.
  • Light! And lots of it. Maximize natural light, and optimize the lighting for the tasks that take place in each space.
  • Grooming and wash stalls designed for ease of use and horse safety.
  • Indoor arenas that are cool in the summer, warm in the winter, and comfortable for friends hanging out.
  • Tack rooms, lounges and feed rooms that are functional and pretty.
  • Summer pastures optimized for rotation and grass health in Colorado’s dry climate.
  • Winter turnouts that are never muddy and stay safe no matter how much it snows.
